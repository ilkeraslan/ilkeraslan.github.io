Why Risk-Based Premiums Will Not Close the Catastrophe Protection Gap

Europe insures only a fraction of its natural catastrophe losses, and the proposed EU scheme relies on risk-based premiums to change that. Once climate change removes a stable loss distribution, catastrophe prices rest on convention, reinsurance capacity moves in cycles and rationing ratchets upward, so pricing alone cannot close the gap.

Review of Political Economy · 2026

Monetary Sovereignty and Catastrophe Fiscal Capacity

Japan absorbed the 2011 earthquake without financial stress, while Turkey's 2023 earthquakes were followed by a sharp fall in the lira. The paper introduces catastrophe fiscal capacity, a state's ability to finance recovery without currency, inflation or debt stress, and traces it to the degree of monetary sovereignty.

    About

    From the underwriting desk to the seminar room

    I am an economist working where insurance markets, financial structures and macroeconomic theory meet.

    Before returning to research and teaching I spent more than a decade in treaty reinsurance in EMEA and international markets, most recently as a senior underwriter for liability, casualty and motor business. That practice shapes the questions I ask: how reinsurance capacity is priced and rationed, how catastrophe and cyber risk reach capital markets, and who absorbs the losses that markets will not carry.

    My research combines quantitative methods with a post-Keynesian theoretical foundation. I hold a doctorate in economics from the University of Fribourg in Switzerland, published as a book by Logos Verlag and reviewed in the Review of Keynesian Economics, a Master of Science in EU Business and Law from Aarhus University in Denmark, and a Bachelor of Science in Business Administration from the University of Texas at Dallas in the United States.

    I live in Zürich with my wife and our two daughters.
