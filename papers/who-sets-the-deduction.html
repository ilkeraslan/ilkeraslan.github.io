Who Sets the Deduction? Central Bank Losses and the Fiscal Cost of Monetary Tightening

Ilker Aslan · Lecturer, Kalaidos University of Applied Sciences, Zürich

Download PDFAll papers

Abstract

When monetary tightening turns central bank results negative, the cost falls on the government, but the deduction central banks place before their transfers decides when it reaches the budget. I code who sets this deduction at 55 central banks and how each loss of 2008–15 and 2022–24 was absorbed. The bank sets it alone at 33 and with the government at ten more; where a bank’s buffer could cover a loss, banks other than the Swiss National Bank released it in 42 of 47 cases. Of the Eurosystem’s losses of 2022–24, equal to 1% of euro-area GDP, 60% was absorbed by buffers withheld in earlier years, 36% was carried forward and 4% was met from public funds. The rule re-times the fiscal cost of tightening rather than changing it. In a New Keynesian model calibrated to the Bundesbank, a Treasury that covers losses at once contracts the budget by 1% of GDP during the tightening and lowers the peak policy rate by 14 basis points; buffers spread the cost over a decade. Coverage is never far from the best rule, so the case for buffers rests on independence, which independence indices do not record.
