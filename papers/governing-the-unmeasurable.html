Governing the Unmeasurable: Rules, Discretion, and the Actuarial Convention

Ilker Aslan · Lecturer, Kalaidos University of Applied Sciences, Zürich

Download PDFAll papers

Abstract

Actuaries certify numbers that no measurement technology delivers: a one-in-two-hundred annual standard no claims record could support, premium risk factors held constant across the underwriting cycle, and, as machine-learned components enter pricing and reserving, outputs whose basis the certifier cannot reconstruct. This paper treats such numbers as conventions in Keynes’s sense and asks how they should be governed. A convention is formalized as a published mapping from a verifiable signal to a binding standard, and rule, discretion and the frozen parameter are compared on time consistency, resistance to capture and auditability. Discretionary revision relaxes the standard where the signal indicates most risk and leaves no record, because the number is the record. Rules trade the frequency of capture for its persistence, so a bound on the mapping’s output limits distortion, and the bound’s optimal width is where the stop-loss premium of the unbounded adjustment equals the capture-weighted bound. Applied to Solvency II, the framework sorts the symmetric adjustment, the 99.5 per cent standard and the premium risk factors into three cells; EIOPA’s daily series since 1991 shows the ±10 corridor binding at three published month-ends in a decade, and widths of 10, 13 and 17 points imply revision-survival probabilities of 0.87, 0.94 and 0.98. A closing section applies the criteria to certifying model outputs that cannot be validated.
