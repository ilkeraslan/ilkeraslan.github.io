Insuring Automation Risk: Reskilling Incentives, Correlated Displacement, and the Price of Aggregate Cover

Ilker Aslan · Lecturer, Kalaidos University of Applied Sciences, Zürich

Download PDFAll papers

Abstract

Automation of an occupation is a common shock to everyone in it, while the ability to move into other work depends on reskilling that an insurer cannot observe. I study insurance against this risk when the common shock is priced by outside capital. The optimal contract has two layers: an index payment to every member of the occupation when it is automated, and a partial indemnity for those who fail to transition. Consumption satisfies an inverse Euler equation across aggregate states, so index cover is rationed although no worker can influence the trigger. Whether mean consumption in the automation state lies below or above the full-insurance rule depends on whether absolute prudence is smaller or larger than twice absolute risk aversion. When workers can hedge privately the rationing cannot be sustained, but the welfare cost is of second order. Under private information about transition prospects the index layer is free of selection and, with decreasing absolute risk aversion, lowers the willingness to pay on which an indemnity market depends. Which layer carries the gains depends on the price of the automation state. From CRSP portfolios sorted on workforce exposure to generative AI, I estimate that price at 0.97 to 0.99 for a holder of the market, statistically indistinguishable from one, and at 0.86 to 0.91 for a holder of the most exposed firms.
